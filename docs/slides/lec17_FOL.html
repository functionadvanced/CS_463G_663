First-Order Logic

First-order logic gives AI a language for talking about objects, relations, and rules that apply broadly.

Why Propositional Logic Is Not Enough

Propositional logic can name whole facts:

\text{HumanSocrates} \rightarrow \text{MortalSocrates}

But it cannot easily express the rule behind the fact:

\forall x\; \text{Human}(x) \rightarrow \text{Mortal}(x)

FOL lets us reason about classes of objects without writing a separate proposition for every object.

Building Blocks of FOL

Building block Meaning Example
Constants Specific objects \text{Socrates}, \text{Alice}, 3
Variables Arbitrary objects x, y, z
Predicates Properties or relations \text{Human}(x), \text{Loves}(\text{Alice}, \text{Bob})
Functions Map objects to objects \text{Father}(\text{John}) = \text{David}
Connectives Combine formulas \land, \lor, \neg, \rightarrow, \leftrightarrow
Quantifiers Specify scope \forall, \exists

Read each formula by asking: what objects exist, what relations hold, and how widely does the claim apply?

Two Core Quantifiers

Universal: \forall

\forall x\; \text{Human}(x) \rightarrow \text{Mortal}(x)

For every object x, if x is human, then x is mortal.

Existential: \exists

\exists x\; \text{Likes}(x, \text{IceCream})

There is at least one object x that likes ice cream.

\forall makes a general rule. \exists promises at least one witness.

Quantifier Order Matters

Everyone Loves Someone

\forall x \exists y\; \text{Loves}(x,y)

Each person may have their own y.

Someone Is Loved by Everyone

\exists y \forall x\; \text{Loves}(x,y)

One same y works for all x.

Swapping the quantifiers changes the claim, even though the predicate is the same.

Translating English to FOL

English sentence FOL pattern
Every student has a teacher. \forall x\; \text{Student}(x) \rightarrow \exists y\; \text{Teacher}(y) \land \text{Teaches}(y,x)
There exists a course that every student takes. \exists c \forall x\; \text{Student}(x) \rightarrow \text{Takes}(x,c)
Every professor teaches at least one course. \forall p\; \text{Professor}(p) \rightarrow \exists c\; \text{Teaches}(p,c)

Choose the domain objects first, then decide where each quantifier must live.

Negating Quantifiers

Negation flips the quantifier as it moves inward:

\neg \forall x\; P(x) \equiv \exists x\; \neg P(x)

\neg \exists x\; P(x) \equiv \forall x\; \neg P(x)

“Not everyone is happy” means:

\neg \forall x\; \text{Happy}(x) \equiv \exists x\; \neg \text{Happy}(x)

Inference Rules in FOL

FOL inference rules derive new facts from quantified knowledge.

Rule Basic idea
Universal Instantiation Replace a universally quantified variable with a specific object.
Existential Instantiation Introduce a new name for an object that must exist.
Generalized Modus Ponens Combine rules and facts after unification.
Resolution Prove by contradiction using clauses and unification.

Substitution and Entailment

Substitution

Substitution replaces variables in a formula:

\theta = \{x / \text{Alice}\}, \qquad \alpha = \text{Loves}(x,y)

\text{SUBST}(\theta,\alpha) = \text{Loves}(\text{Alice},y)

Entailment

\text{KB} \models \alpha

means \alpha is true in every model where \text{KB} is true.

UI and EI

Universal Instantiation

From:

\forall x\; P(x)

infer:

P(a)

for any object a.

Existential Instantiation

From:

\exists x\; P(x)

introduce a fresh constant c and infer:

P(c)

EI needs a new name; otherwise the proof may accidentally assume which object exists.

Unification

Unification finds a substitution \theta that makes expressions match.

Given:

\text{Knows}(\text{John},x) \qquad \text{Knows}(\text{John},\text{Bob})

A unifier is:

\theta = \{x / \text{Bob}\}

Unification is the matching step that lets FOL inference use general rules with specific facts.

Generalized Modus Ponens

Rule

P_1 \land P_2 \land \cdots \land P_k \rightarrow Q

If the facts match the premises under \theta, infer:

\text{SUBST}(\theta,Q)

Example

\forall x\; \text{Human}(x) \rightarrow \text{Mortal}(x)

\text{Human}(\text{Socrates})

\theta = \{x / \text{Socrates}\}

\text{Mortal}(\text{Socrates})

Conjunctive Normal Form

CNF is a conjunction of clauses; each clause is a disjunction of literals.

(P \lor Q \lor \neg R) \land (\neg P \lor R) \land Q

Literal

An atom or its negation:

P, \quad \neg R

Clause

A disjunction of literals:

P \lor Q \lor \neg R

Converting FOL to CNF

Use CNF because resolution works over clauses.

  1. Eliminate implications: A \rightarrow B \equiv \neg A \lor B.
  1. Move negations inward: apply De Morgan’s laws and quantifier negation.
  1. Standardize variables: rename bound variables to avoid collisions.
  1. Skolemize: replace existential variables with Skolem constants or functions.
  1. Drop universal quantifiers and distribute \lor over \land.

CNF Example: Start

“Everyone who loves all animals is loved by someone.”

\forall x\left( (\forall y(\text{Animal}(y) \rightarrow \text{Loves}(x,y))) \rightarrow (\exists z\; \text{Loves}(z,x)) \right)

The outer statement says: if x loves all animals, then someone loves x.

CNF Example: Transform

Remove implications

\forall x\left( \neg \forall y(\neg \text{Animal}(y) \lor \text{Loves}(x,y)) \lor \exists z\; \text{Loves}(z,x) \right)

Move negation inward

\forall x\left( \exists y(\text{Animal}(y) \land \neg \text{Loves}(x,y)) \lor \exists z\; \text{Loves}(z,x) \right)

CNF Example: Clauses

Skolemize

\forall x\left( (\text{Animal}(f(x)) \land \neg \text{Loves}(x,f(x))) \lor \text{Loves}(g(x),x) \right)

Distribute

(\text{Animal}(f(x)) \lor \text{Loves}(g(x),x)) \land (\neg \text{Loves}(x,f(x)) \lor \text{Loves}(g(x),x))

Resolution in Propositional Logic

Resolution eliminates complementary literals:

\frac{(P \lor A),\quad(\neg P \lor B)}{A \lor B}

(\neg \text{Rain} \lor \text{Wet}), \quad \text{Rain}

Resolving on \text{Rain} gives:

\text{Wet}

Resolution in FOL

FOL resolution adds unification before resolving.

\text{Loves}(x,\text{Alice}) \lor \text{Hates}(x,\text{Bob})

\neg \text{Loves}(\text{John},\text{Alice}) \lor \text{Friends}(\text{John},\text{Bob})

Unify with \theta = \{x / \text{John}\}, then resolve on \text{Loves}(\text{John},\text{Alice}).

\text{Hates}(\text{John},\text{Bob}) \lor \text{Friends}(\text{John},\text{Bob})

What to Remember

  • FOL represents objects, properties, relations, functions, and quantified rules.
  • Quantifier order is part of the meaning.
  • Substitution and unification connect general rules to specific facts.
  • CNF prepares formulas for resolution.
  • Resolution is a proof engine for automated reasoning.
