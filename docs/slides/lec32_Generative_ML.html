Generative Machine Learning

Generative models learn to create new data that resembles the training distribution.

What Is Generative ML?

Generative ML focuses on modeling:

p_\theta(x)

so that samples from p_\theta look like samples from the real data distribution p(x).

Applications:

  • Image, video, music, and text generation.
  • Drug discovery and materials science.
  • Game assets and virtual environments.
  • Data augmentation.

AI, ML, DL, Generative ML

Generative ML is not separate from ML; it is a family of learning problems focused on producing samples.

Generative vs. Discriminative

Model type Learns Example output
Discriminative p(y\mid x) or a decision boundary “cat” vs. “dog”
Generative p(x) or p(x\mid c) a new image, sentence, molecule

Classification asks “what label?” Generation asks “what could a realistic example look like?”

Training Objective

Goal:

p_\theta(x)\approx p(x)

Common objective:

\mathcal{L}(\theta)= \mathbb{E}_{x\sim p(x)} [-\log p_\theta(x)]

Equivalently:

\mathcal{L}(\theta)=D_{KL}(p(x)\parallel p_\theta(x))+\text{const.}

Training pushes the model distribution toward the data distribution.

Evaluation Is Hard

Generative outputs are high-dimensional and often stochastic.

Why simple accuracy does not work:

  • There may be many valid outputs.
  • Exact match can be meaningless.
  • Human perception matters.

Common metrics:

  • FID for images.
  • BLEU for text.
  • Perceptual similarity.
  • Human evaluation.

Model Families

Family Core idea
Autoencoders / VAEs Encode data into latent variables, then decode
GANs Generator competes with discriminator
Autoregressive models Predict next token/pixel from previous ones
Normalizing flows Invertible transformations of simple distributions
Energy-based models Assign low energy to realistic samples
Diffusion models Learn to reverse a noising process
Transformers Sequence modeling backbone for many generative systems

Autoencoders

Purpose:

  • Compress input into a representation.
  • Reconstruct the input.
  • Learn useful latent features.

Autoencoder vs. VAE Encoder

Autoencoder

Encoder maps input to one latent point:

z=f_\theta(x)

Variational autoencoder

Encoder maps input to a distribution:

q_\theta(z\mid x)=\mathcal{N}(\mu,\sigma^2)

VAEs make latent space smoother and easier to sample from.

Diffusion Models

Denoising diffusion models generate data by learning to reverse a gradual noising process.

  • Forward process: add noise.
  • Reverse process: remove noise step by step.
  • Sampling starts from random noise.

Diffusion: Forward and Reverse

Diffusion Paths

Forward noising:

x_0 \rightarrow x_1 \rightarrow \cdots \rightarrow x_T

Reverse denoising during generation:

\tilde{x}_T \rightarrow \tilde{x}_{T-1} \rightarrow \cdots \rightarrow \tilde{x}_0

The model learns many small denoising steps instead of one huge generation step.

What to Remember

  • Generative models learn to produce realistic new samples.
  • The target is a distribution, not just a label.
  • Evaluation is difficult because many outputs can be valid.
  • VAEs, GANs, autoregressive models, flows, and diffusion models are major families.
  • Diffusion models generate by reversing noise.
